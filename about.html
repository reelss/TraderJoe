What is Joe?

Joe is a fully autonomous AI trading agent running on a $10,000 paper (simulated) account. He scans financial news and market data every hour during market hours, reasons over the candidates like a disciplined swing trader, and places real orders through Alpaca's paper trading API — all without any human intervention.

The brain is Claude (Anthropic's AI). Every decision Joe makes is logged verbatim — the reasoning, the conviction, the outcome. This is an experiment in whether a well-prompted LLM with good market data can trade profitably over time.

The Strategy

01
Swing trading. Joe holds positions for days to weeks — never chases intraday moves. He is not a day trader.
02
Trend filter. Only opens new longs when SPY is above its 200-day SMA. If the broad market is in a downtrend, Joe manages existing positions and waits.
03
VIX fear filter. When market fear (VIX) is elevated (>20), position sizes are cut. When fear is extreme (>25), no new entries at all.
04
Triple confirmation. A buy requires: price above 20 & 50-day SMA, bullish MACD, and RSI between 50–75. All three, or no trade.
05
Momentum ranking. Among valid setups, Joe prioritises names with the strongest 12-month relative momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman factor).
06
Volume confirmation. Entries are preferred when today's volume is 1.5× the 20-day average. A breakout on low volume is suspect.
07
Earnings avoidance. Joe never enters a new position within 5 days of earnings. Holding through a binary event is a coin flip, not a swing trade.
08
Sector diversification. No single sector can exceed 30% of invested equity. Correlated bets amplify losses.
09
ATR-based risk sizing. Each position is sized so entry-to-stop risk equals ~1% of equity. Volatile names get smaller positions and wider stops automatically.
10
Trailing stops. Once a position peaks at +7.5%, the stop rises to breakeven — the trade becomes zero-risk. Once it peaks at +10%, the floor locks in +5% profit. Winners are protected automatically without giving back all the gains.
11
52-week high proximity. Joe prefers names trading within 15% of their 52-week high — they carry the least overhead resistance. Names more than 30% below their highs are avoided unless there is a compelling catalyst.
12
Relative strength vs. SPY. Each candidate is measured against SPY's 20-day return. Joe prioritises names outperforming the index. If a held position lags SPY by more than 5% over 20 days, it is flagged for exit — capital belongs in leaders, not laggards.
13
PDT-aware. On a sub-$25k account, pattern day trading rules cap same-day round trips. Joe never sells a position opened the same day.
14
Intraday context. Joe sees real-time prices, not just yesterday's close. If a name is already up >10% today on the discovery news, it's likely extended — Joe requires higher conviction before entering. A stock down >5% today without explanation is flagged as a caution before buying into weakness.
15
Macro calendar awareness. Joe checks for upcoming high-impact US events (Fed rate decisions, CPI, NFP, GDP) in the next 5 days. When a major macro event is ≤2 days away, Joe tightens stops, prefers smaller position sizes, and notes the event explicitly in his reasoning.
16
Insider buying signal. Joe checks SEC Form 4 filings for open-market purchases by executives and directors. When insiders buy their own stock in the open market — putting personal capital in at current prices — it is a high-conviction positive signal. Used as a tiebreaker: it raises conviction by one level when other signals are already constructive.
17
Partial exits. Joe can scale out of positions rather than exit all-or-nothing. When a position is up significantly with an intact trend, Joe may sell half to bank gains and let the remainder run with a tight trailing stop — rather than waiting for either a full take-profit or a reversal.
18
Market breadth filter. Joe monitors how many of the 11 major sector ETFs are above their 200-day moving averages. When fewer than 45% of sectors are trending up, the market's internal health is deteriorating even if the S&P 500 index itself hasn't rolled over yet — a leading warning before the headline index breaks.
19
Stale position exits. Capital that isn't moving is wasted capital. When a position has been held for 21+ days with less than 4% gain, Joe sees a stale flag and must justify continued holding with a specific near-term catalyst — not hope. A hard auto-exit fires unconditionally at 35 days with less than 2% gain. This is how professional traders enforce capital discipline: dead money gets recycled into names with actual momentum.
20
Re-entry cooldown. When a position is stopped out, Joe cannot re-enter that same stock for 5 days. A failed breakout reveals something about the stock's supply and demand — re-buying immediately is often buying the same weakness twice. The pause forces Joe to wait for the stock to build a new base before re-entering.

How Joe Learns

Joe has two learning loops running in parallel, both fed with structured performance data:

DAILY
Nightly reflection. After every market close, Sonnet reviews the day's decisions and trade outcomes — including entry price, exit price, and hold time — plus rolling 30-day win rate, P&L distributions, and exit-reason breakdowns. It also reviews counterfactual lessons: names Joe passed on and what they did afterward (missed gains and correct passes). The output is a rewritten playbook Joe reads before every hourly decision.
WEEKLY
Sunday strategy review. Each Sunday, Sonnet takes a wider view — 7 and 30-day performance stats, sector rotation, macro regime — and produces a strategy document with sector tilts, a watchlist for the week ahead, and directional adjustments. Joe reads this too, every hour.
BACKTEST
Historical validation. Joe's strategy rules can be backtested against years of historical data — no LLM calls, fully deterministic, fills at next-day open with slippage. Outputs Sharpe ratio, CAGR, max drawdown, win rate, and per-exit-reason breakdowns. Used to validate signal edge and tune stop thresholds before trading with real capital.
